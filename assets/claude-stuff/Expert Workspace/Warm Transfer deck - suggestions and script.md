# Warm Transfer deck: suggestions and script

Deck: [Warm Handoff Story — Design Team](https://www.figma.com/slides/SV29gHepJ1HAYMtWRyyc8t)
Target: about 5 minutes, about 600 spoken words.

## Fix before presenting

- Slide 5: "coundn't" → "couldn't", and "saliling" → "sailing".
- Slide 10: "We piloted and / and launched it" repeats "and".
- Slide 2 has two versions of the body copy stacked on top of each other. Keep one.
- Pick one name. The cover says "Warm Transfer", but slides 13 and 14 still say "warm handoff" / "bot handoff".
- Cover badge: "CONVO IVR + EXPERT WORKSPACE" is all caps. Use sentence case: "Convo IVR + Expert Workspace".
- Capitalization: write "Asurion Expert" the first time, then "expert" lowercase after that. Slide 2 capitalizes "Experts".

## Flow changes

1. **Show the problem before the solution.** The greeting card (slide 4) comes before "the catch," so the audience sees the answer before the question. Move it after "What experts get in those seconds." That way it lands as "here's what that looks like."
2. **Move the Jason slide (16) up.** Right now it's after the Q&A slide, so nobody will see it. It's the perfect setup for "Experts told us they want this," so put it right before that slide.
3. **Cut or explain slide 8 (the workspace dashboard).** It shows "available for calls in 24s" and stats, not the transfer. If it's meant to be the "take a breath" moment, add a headline that says so. Otherwise, cut it.
4. **Cut the timeline (slide 11).** It repeats "we shipped it," and its "46 sec faster calls" card gives away your big reveal two slides early. If you keep it, change the Oct card to "Results came in → Scale it".
5. **Bring back a thank-you to Jason.** Add it as a line on the Next slide, or as a slide before Q&A. Jason has left, and this is the room that should hear it.

## Copy edits, slide by slide

**Cover**
- Badge: Convo IVR + Expert Workspace

**What is Warm Transfer?**
- Body: "When Joy can't solve it, she hands the customer to an Asurion Expert, and the context comes with them."
- One sentence is enough. The Joy → expert visual already explains the rest.

**Where we left off at Asurion NEXT → "What we showed at Asurion NEXT"**
- The cards work as they are. Optional tweak to card 3: "Joy hands it to an expert → Nobody starts over".

**The catch**
- Eyebrow: "One catch" (replaces "It's not all smooth sailing", which is a cliché and longer).
- Headline: "Joy, the customer, and the expert can't all be on the line at once."

**Prep time:** keep as is. It's the strongest line in the deck.

**What experts get in those seconds**
- "Say the right greeting" → sub: "Their script, with their name in it" (more concrete than "a consistent and personalized script").

**Greeting card (moved):** add a headline, "What the expert sees", or let it stand alone while you narrate.

**Jason slide**
- Eyebrow: "Before launch, Jason tested it with experts on floor 5."
- Headline: keep "One of the most positive feedback sessions he's ever run."

**Experts told us they want this:** keep. This is the clearest slide.

**Shipped slide**
- "So we shipped it." Put "shipped it." in gradient.
- Or: "Then it went live." with sub "Verizon onboarding · Sep 30".

**The pilot beat the forecast:** keep. Say the "0 sec" card out loud, because it's what sets up the reveal.

**−46 sec**
- Subline: "faster per call in the pilot".

## Script (about 5 min)

**1 · Cover**
Quick one today: Warm Transfer. It's Convo IVR and Expert Workspace working together, and the early results are better than any of us expected.

**2 · What is Warm Transfer?**
Customers talk to Joy, our conversational IVR. When Joy can't solve it, she hands the customer to an expert, and the context comes with them.

**3 · What we showed at Asurion NEXT**
At NEXT we showed the vision. The customer tells Joy what's wrong, in their own words. Joy figures out the issue. Then she hands it to an expert, so nobody starts over.

**4 · One catch**
Here's the catch. Technically, Joy, the customer, and the expert can't all be on the line at once. So there's a gap: Joy is wrapping up with the customer while the expert just… waits.

**5 · Prep time**
So we turned that wait into prep time.

**6 · What experts get**
In those few seconds, the expert gets ready. They see why the customer is calling. They get the right greeting, with their name already in it. And they get a breath after the last call.

**7 · Greeting card**
Here's what that looks like. Who's calling, what they need, and a greeting ready to read the second they're live.

**8 · Jason's session**
Before launch, Jason took the prototype to experts on floor 5. He called it one of the most positive feedback sessions he's ever run.

**9 · Experts want this**
Three things stood out. First, they finally get a moment to compose. Today they say they feel randomly thrown onto calls. Second, the greeting is right there, no digging through Helix. Third, they loved the new tone over today's whisper.

**10 · Shipped**
So we shipped it. The pilot went live on Verizon onboarding at the end of September.

**11 · The pilot beat the forecast**
Here's the setup: 800 experts, about 200 calls a day. And going in, analytics forecast zero CRT gain. This was supposed to be an experience win, not a speed win.

**12 · −46 sec**
*(Pause.)* Instead, calls got up to 46 seconds faster.

**13 · Next**
Next, we're pushing to 100% of Verizon onboarding calls. Then the rest of Verizon, AT&T once their IVR migration wraps, and eventually Joy staying on the line for a true three-way intro.

And a big thank you to Jason, who got this off the ground and isn't here to hear it.

**14 · Questions**
That's it. Any questions?

## Delivery tips

- Say the 46 out loud and let it sit for a beat. It's the only number people need to remember.
- Don't read slide text word for word. Most slides are a single line, so the script adds to them rather than repeating them.
- If you're running long, cut slide 3. The cover visual and slide 2 already explain the flow.
