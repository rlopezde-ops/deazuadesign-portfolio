# In-Store Claims Kiosk — Case Study Copy
> Apple-style presentation structure. One idea per slide. Large text. Minimal copy. Self-explanatory without narration.

---

## SLIDE 1 — Title

**Bridging the Gap Between**
**Repair Stores and Claims**

Principal Designer · Asurion · 2021–2022

---

## SLIDE 2 — Setup

**In 2019, Asurion began rebranding uBreakiFix stores.**

Customers started walking in expecting help
with their insurance claims.

---

## SLIDE 3 — The Problem

**Stores weren't equipped to help.**

Employees aren't licensed insurance adjusters —
filing a claim on a customer's behalf could be classified as fraud.
Store systems didn't connect to the claims platform.

---

## SLIDE 4 — Business Challenge

**Technicians spent up to 45 minutes per customer**
**trying to help — time they couldn't afford.**

Staffing was already stretched thin.
The gap between brand promise and in-store reality was widening.

---

## SLIDE 5 — Research

**In-store visits. Technician interviews. Direct feedback from owners.**

The pain surfaced fast — and consistently.

---

## SLIDE 6 — Voice of the Customer

**Three versions of the same confusion**

> "I have an Asurion protection plan and saw your store on my way to work. Can you fix my phone?"

> "I need to get my screen fixed, but I don't remember if I have a protection plan."

> "I think I completed my claim, but I'm not sure. Can you help me with it?"

---

## SLIDE 7 — The Expert's Dilemma

**The only answer technicians could give:**

> "You need to call Asurion, your carrier, or go to phoneclaim.com"

Even with the customer standing right in front of them.

---

## SLIDE 8 — What Was Actually Broken

- **Regulatory & Compliance Barriers** — techs legally couldn't act on a customer's claim
- **Disconnected Systems** — stores had zero visibility into claim status
- **Technicians Spread Thin** — 45 minutes per customer, staffing already stretched

**The problem wasn't willingness. It was structure.**

---

## SLIDE 9 — Design Principles

**Simple. Empowering. Efficient.**

Simplify the in-store claims experience for everyone.
Explore self-service tools that give customers a next step.
Maximize technicians' time on repairs, not paperwork.

---

## SLIDE 10 — The Approach

**Service blueprints to align stakeholders at odds.**

A current-state blueprint diagnosed where the journey broke down —
so every stakeholder saw the same facts.

A future-state blueprint gave us a shared target to design toward.

---

## SLIDE 11 — The Idea

**A warm handoff, not a hard stop.**

Give customers an easy way to file and manage claims
without compliance issues — by handing them off
to an in-store tablet for self-service.

---

## SLIDE 12 — The 20-Store Pilot

**Design and operations disagreed on the form factor.**

Design advocated for handheld tablets —
familiar from Apple Store and Warby Parker.

Operations prioritized theft concerns and wanted
customers to self-serve at a standing kiosk.

---

## SLIDE 13 — What the Pilot Showed

**Customers didn't realize they could use the kiosk alone.**

Technicians kept walking over to help them get started anyway —
proof that self-service alone wasn't holding up on its own.

**That evidence, not opinion, resolved the disagreement.**

---

## SLIDE 14 — The Solution

**Knowledge is power. Confidence is key.**

Technicians quickly identify if a customer has a protection plan
and guide them to file or resume a claim on the tablet.

We removed unnecessary steps — location defaults to the current store,
identity verification simplified since the customer is present.

---

## SLIDE 15 — Solution Features

- **Knowledge is Power** — technicians see plan status instantly
- **Build Confidence** — customers get clear guidance on their device issue
- **Warm Handoff** — the human connection stays intact through the transition

---

## SLIDE 16 — Evolution: Kiosk to Handheld

**After the pilot, we scaled with an updated handheld tablet.**

A collaborative call the full team made together —
grounded in what we saw in stores, not preference.

**The handheld flow created a more personal, 1:1 experience —
the best of both worlds.**

---

## SLIDE 17 — My Role

**Principal designer, end to end.**

Research planning and in-store interviews with the Director of User Research.
Led service design workshops across operations, stores, and product.
Partnered with marketing on kiosk signage and tablet motion design.
Owned pilot strategy from 20 stores to full corporate rollout.

---

## SLIDE 18 — Impact

**From 45 minutes to under 10.**

- **<10 min** claim resolution time, down from 45
- **10,000+** customers helped per month
- **$100K+** saved in call and chat deflection

---

## SLIDE 19 — What I'd Do Differently / Lessons

**The best solutions come from the frontline, not the desk.**

Co-creation workshops, store pilots, and continuous feedback
were what aligned stakeholders who started out at odds.

**When everyone sees the same map of the problem,
solutions emerge collaboratively — and buy-in comes naturally.**

---

## SLIDE 20 — Closing

# The technicians couldn't file the claim.

## So we gave the customer a way to do it themselves —
## warmly, in under 10 minutes, without ever feeling alone.
