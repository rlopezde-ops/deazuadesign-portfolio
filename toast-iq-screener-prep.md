# Toast IQ — Principal Product Designer
## Screener Call Prep · Ricardo De Azúa

---

### Q1. Tell me about yourself.

"So I've got about twenty plus years of design experience, but I've spent the last ten years designing products for customers and the people who serve them — think hospitality, operations, and support. High-stakes environments where getting it wrong actually hurts the business and the customer.

At Universal Orlando, I led consumer-facing work — the Halloween Horror Nights redesign, which drove double-digit growth in ticket sales. At Disney Parks, I built an internal tool for Cast Members that replaced about 60 disconnected apps with one unified experience and improved their efficiency by about 25%. That's really where I internalized something: the bar for internal tools has to be the same as consumer products. The people using them are the ones delivering your brand and your brand promise.

From there I went to Asurion, which is where I am today, and I've been leading design at scale. I rebuilt the POS for 680+ uBreakiFix repair stores, and right now I'm leading an AI-powered tool for 10,000+ call center agents — we call it Sidecar. It's a proactive copilot that listens to live calls and guides agents to the next best action without them ever having to ask for it.

And that's honestly what drew me to the Toast IQ role. It's the same problem I've been working on — how do you give someone in a high-pressure moment exactly what they need, and make it feel effortless?"

---

### Q2. Why Toast, and why this role specifically?

"Honestly, it's the product problem. Toast IQ is trying to do what I'm already doing at Asurion — putting a proactive AI in the hands of someone in a high-pressure operational moment and making it feel effortless. The biggest difference is the scale Toast is operating at. I'm designing for 10,000 call center agents — Toast is at 170,000+ locations, with rich data across menus, labor, and transactions. That's an incredibly powerful foundation to design on top of. And I'm particularly excited about where the product is headed — the shift from reactive chat to agentic workflows, designing how those specialist agents coordinate. That's the frontier I want to be working on. I also have a former colleague at Toast who's been really positive about the culture, which never hurts."

---

### Q3. Walk me through your current role.

"I'm currently a senior IC leading design on Expert Workspace — Asurion's AI-powered tool for 10,000+ call center and chat agents. The core problem we're trying to solve is: how do you take a high-pressure support call and give the agent exactly what they need, when they need it, without having to think about what's next — giving them the next best step regardless of where they are in a call.

Two things I'm working on that I'm really proud of: we shipped a visual call pacing feature that reduced average call length by about two minutes, and at the scale we're operating at, every minute saved is roughly a million dollars. And we're piloting an AI-powered pitch tool that generates compliant, natural-sounding upsell scripts in real time based on the conversation — early results are showing a 2% lift in sales.

A lot of this work started with field research — visiting call centers in Orlando and talking to agents in the Philippines — because you can't design for a high-pressure environment without being in it. Overall, my approach is always future vision first, then work backwards. We're not building incrementally — we're building intentionally."

---

### Q4. Tell me about a time you designed something that had measurable impact.

"A great example is the store POS system I led at Asurion — an internal tool used across almost 700 uBreakiFix locations. The old system required about two weeks of training for new technicians. After we redesigned it from the ground up with a guided, intuitive experience — consumer-quality UX applied to an internal tool — we cut that to three days. That's about an 85% reduction in training time, which at that scale translates directly to faster store readiness and lower onboarding costs. The lesson for me was that the bar for internal tools has to be the same as consumer products. If someone needs two weeks to learn your tool, that's a design failure — not a training problem."

---

### Q5. What's your experience designing AI or data-driven products?

"My deepest AI design experience is what I'm building now — we call it Sidecar. It's a proactive AI copilot that listens to live support calls and surfaces the next best action in real time, without the agent ever having to ask for it. The core design insight was that a call center agent can't type into a tool mid-conversation — that breaks the human moment they're having with the customer. So the AI has to do the listening and the thinking, and get out of the way. That constraint completely changes how you design the interface. It's not a chat window — it's ambient guidance. Knowing when to surface something, how much to show, and when to stay silent — that's the central design challenge. That's the AI UX muscle I've been building, and it's directly applicable to what Toast IQ is trying to do."

---

### Q6. How do you work with product and engineering?

"I work with a low-ego, blurred-boundaries approach — the best solution wins. A good example is visual call pacing. That feature actually started with an engineer who prototyped the core idea directly in the codebase. The execution wasn't quite at the craft level we needed, but the hypothesis was solid. So rather than starting over in Figma, I jumped into the codebase with them, refined it in place using Cursor, and we shipped something that felt fully integrated into the system. Product had the insight, engineering proved the concept, I elevated the craft — and we ended up with the two-minute call reduction I mentioned earlier. That kind of overlap is how I prefer to work. Designers can code, engineers can prototype — as long as everyone's aligned on the vision and the quality bar."

At Asurion I've had real ownership and high craft standards, and I've built things that reach tens of thousands of people — but I'm ready for a bigger stage. Toast is interesting to me because it operates at massive scale — 170,000+ locations — but still moves and thinks like a modern product company. And there's still a ton of room to grow, especially with Toast IQ. I'd be working on something that's central to the company's future, not a side initiative. That combination of scale, growth potential, and product ambition is exactly what I'm looking for."

---

### Q9. Tell me about your design philosophy or approach to design leadership. What does good design mean to you, and how do you lead your team around that?

"Good design, to me, is when something works so well that it disappears — the user never thinks about the tool, they just accomplish what they're trying to do. That's the bar I hold myself and my team to, especially when we're building for people in high-pressure moments.

My leadership approach is about creating the conditions for the best idea to win, regardless of where it comes from. Low-ego teams with blurred boundaries between design, product, and engineering — designers can code, engineers can prototype. As long as we're aligned on the vision and the quality bar, we move fast and iterate together.

A recent example is visual call pacing. An engineer had the core insight and prototyped it in code. Rather than starting over in Figma, I jumped into the codebase with them using Cursor and refined it in place. We shipped something fully integrated and got a two-minute call reduction. That kind of trust and overlap is how I prefer to work.

I also lead vision-first — get aligned on where we're taking the product, then work backwards. That way we're iterating strategically, not just iterating into a corner."

---

### Q10. You're going to be working on Toast IQ, which is a restaurant operations platform. How much do you know about the restaurant industry, and how would you approach learning that domain?

"My restaurant experience is entirely as a consumer — I love a great restaurant experience, but I've never worked in one. Honestly, I think that's an advantage early on. I come in without preconceptions about how things 'should' work, which keeps me focused on what users actually need rather than what the industry assumes they need.

My approach has always been immersion. Before Asurion, I'd never repaired a phone. So I went into stores, observed technicians, interviewed them, and mapped their day-to-day before designing anything. I did the same with call centers — spent multiple days on-site in Orlando and with agents in the Philippines. That fieldwork is what made the difference between a tool that looks good and one that actually works under pressure.

I'd do exactly that with restaurants — spend time with operators and managers on the floor, during service, understanding their jobs to be done in real conditions. The goal is always the same: help the people in the trenches deliver a great customer experience. That through line is what I know deeply, regardless of the domain."
