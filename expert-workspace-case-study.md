**Leading the AI Experience Vision**
**for Expert Workspace**

## SLIDE 2 — Setup

**Expert Workspace had everything.**

A powerful set of tools.
Every feature solving a real problem.

---

## SLIDE 3 — The Problem

**Together, they created a fragmented experience.**

Experts spent more time navigating tools
than helping customers.

---

## SLIDE 4 — Business Challenge

**Leadership wasn't asking us to redesign the product.**

> "How do we improve solve rate, sell rate,
> and customer experience?"

The answer wasn't another feature.

---

## SLIDE 5 — Research

**We started with experts.**

Two days. Orlando call center.
No assumptions. No wireframes.

We went to observe.

---

## SLIDE 6 — Research Activities

**What we did**

- Call listening
- Expert and coach interviews
- Workflow observation
- Journey mapping
- Future-state workshops

---

## SLIDE 7 — Insight 1

# Experts were doing tool gymnastics.

> "The tools aren't connected."

Workspace → ACSS → Ozmo → Knowledge base → Browser tabs.
Every switch increased hold time, after-call work, and errors.

**The problem wasn't a lack of tools.
It was a lack of orchestration.**

---

## SLIDE 8 — Insight 2

# AI waited to be asked.

> "I go to Expert Assist after troubleshooting...
> it repeats the same steps. I feel stuck."

Experts had to decide when to use AI,
which tool to open, what to type,
and whether to trust the answer.

**AI wasn't reducing effort.
It was creating another decision.**

---

## SLIDE 9 — Insight 3

# Experts had already seen the future.

In an earlier pilot, AI automatically listened,
captured issues, suggested next steps,
and updated as the call evolved.

Experts consistently wanted that back.

**They weren't asking for a better chatbot.
They wanted an AI teammate.**

---

## SLIDE 10 — The Turning Point

# What if AI became the workspace...

## ...instead of another tool inside it?

---

## SLIDE 11 — The Shift

**One question changed every conversation.**

Instead of asking:
*"Where should this feature go?"*

We started asking:
*"What does the expert need right now?"*

---

## SLIDE 12 — Design Principles

**The experience wasn't missing functionality.**
**It was missing guidance.**

- **Focus Attention** — One clear place to look
- **Guide Proactively** — Surface the next best action
- **Build Trust** — Explain recommendations. Stay consistent.
- **Stay in the Conversation** — Reduce searching. Increase human connection.

---

## SLIDE 13 — Strategy

**A complete redesign wasn't the right move.**

We built a roadmap that delivered value immediately
while moving toward a larger transformation.

**Phase 1 → Morning Paper**
Prepare experts before every interaction.

**Phase 2 → Sidecar**
Guide experts throughout every conversation.

**North Star → AI Teammate**
Understands context. Anticipates needs. Orchestrates work.

---

## SLIDE 14 — Morning Paper

# Start every shift with clarity.

Before Morning Paper, experts started cold.
No context. No priorities. No preparation.

Morning Paper changed that.

- What should I focus on today?
- Where do I need coaching?
- What's coming next?

**Not a dashboard. The first step toward trusting proactive AI.**

---

## SLIDE 15 — Morning Paper — Design Decisions

**Key choices that shaped the experience**

We tested three approaches:
a notification model, a full dashboard, and a briefing format.

Experts responded to the briefing.
It felt like a teammate catching them up —
not a system reporting data.

That distinction guided every design decision that followed.

---

## SLIDE 16 — Sidecar

# Stay with the expert.

If Morning Paper prepared experts for the day,
Sidecar was with them for every conversation.

- Live transcript
- Real-time AI guidance
- Customer context
- Guided troubleshooting
- Suggested next actions

**AI that understood the conversation as it happened.**
**No prompts. No searching. No switching.**

---

## SLIDE 17 — The Transformation

**Before**

Search → Switch → Prompt → Wait → Verify

**After**

See → Understand → Guide → Act → Help

## SLIDE 18 — Impact

**Early results validated the direction.**

- Experts onboarded to Morning Paper reported faster call preparation
- Reduced reliance on external knowledge tabs during Sidecar pilots
- Cross-functional teams aligned on a shared AI experience roadmap for the first time
- Design principles adopted across adjacent Expert Workspace initiatives

*Quantitative measurement ongoing as pilots scale.*

---

## SLIDE 20 — What I'd Do Differently

**The lessons that matter at this level**

**Vision alone doesn't move organizations.**
Pairing the North Star with an incremental roadmap was what created executive buy-in.
People needed to see the first step before committing to the destination.

**Prototypes are alignment tools.**
The most productive cross-functional conversations happened around a working demo,
not a strategy doc.

**Ship to learn, not to finish.**
Morning Paper wasn't the end goal.
It was evidence. The best way to fund the next phase of the vision.
